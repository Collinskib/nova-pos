Offline POS Explained: How Local-First Tills Work

ShujaaPOS · November 2026 · 5 min read

"Offline-first" sounds like marketing. It's actually a specific way of building software, and understanding it helps you ask better questions when you're choosing a POS.

The classic setup

Most software you use is thin. The app on your screen is a window; the real work happens on a server far away. Gmail works this way. So do most POS systems: the till displays buttons, but the sale is written to a database in a data centre. Cut the connection and the window shows you nothing.

The local-first setup

An offline-first till flips that. The machine in your shop holds its own copy of the database. Selling, searching products, checking stock, printing a receipt: all of that is done right there, fast, with no network at all.

Then there's a second piece: a sync engine running quietly in the background. Its whole job is to keep the local copy and the cloud copy telling the same story.

How the sync actually works

In our build, and this is a common pattern, every change becomes an event. A sale is an event. A new product is an event. A stock adjustment is an event. Events get numbered, queued, pushed to the cloud when a path exists, and acknowledged when they land. If two devices record changes, each carries enough identity (device, business, timestamp) to sort out who did what.

That gives you a few quiet guarantees:

  • Nothing is lost in a dropout. Events wait in a durable queue on the device itself.
  • Nothing is double-counted. Duplicate pushes are detected by event ID and skipped.
  • Every device sees the same catalog. Product events flow down the same channel sales events flow up.

Why this matters for a shop in Kenya

Fibre here is better than it was, but it still drops. Power flickers. Routers get unplugged by cleaners. A business that depends on a live connection to take money is fragile in a way a business shouldn't be. Local-first means the internet is a bonus, not a dependency.

The one question to ask any vendor: "Show me a completed sale with the Wi-Fi off, then show me it appearing in reports when you reconnect."
